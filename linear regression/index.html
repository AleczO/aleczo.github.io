Useful theorems

Theorem 1

$$ \int_{0}^{2a} f(x) dx = \int_{0}^{a} f(x) dx + \int_{0}^{a} f(2a - x) dx $$

Theorem 2

$$ \int_{0}^{a} f(x) dx = \int_{0}^{a} f(a - x) dx $$

Theorem 3

For continuous even functions

$$ \int_{-a}^{a} f(x) dx = 2\int_{0}^{a} f(x) dx $$

$$ \int \frac{(x-1)^{\log{(x+1)}}} {(x+1)^{\log{(x-1)}}} dx $$ $$ = \int \frac{e^{\log({(x-1)}^{\log{(x+1)}})}}{e^{\log({(x+1)}^{\log{(x-1)}})}} dx $$ $$ = \int \frac{e^{\log{(x+1)}\log{(x-1)}}}{e^{\log{(x-1)}\log{(x+1)}}} dx $$ $$ = \int \frac{e^{\log{(x+1)}}e^{\log{(x-1)}}}{e^{\log{(x-1)}}e^{\log{(x+1)}}} dx $$ $$ = \int 1 dx $$ $$ = x + C $$

$$ \int (x \log{x} + 2x) dx = $$ $$ = \int x\log{x} dx + \int 2x dx $$

The first integral can be solved using integration by parts $$ \int x\log{x} dx = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \log{x} - \frac{1}{2}\int x dx = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \log{x} - \frac{1}{4}x^2 $$

Second integral $$ \int 2x dx = x^2 $$

Now substituting solutions $$ = \int x\log{x} dx + \int 2x dx $$ $$ = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \log{x} - \frac{1}{4}x^2 + x^2 = $$ $$ = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \log{x} + \frac{3}{4}x^2 + C $$

$$ \int \frac{dx}{x\log{x} + 2x} = \int \frac{dx}{x(\log{x} + 2)} $$

We can use substitution $$ t = \log{x} + 2 $$ $$ dt = \frac{1}{x} dx $$ $$ = \int \frac{dt}{t} = \log{|t|} + C $$
$$ = \log{(\log{x} + 2)} + C $$

$$ \int \frac{\cos x + \cot x + \csc x + 1}{\sin x + \tan x + \sec x + 1} dx $$

rewrite the numerator

$$ \cos x + \cot x + \csc x + 1 = $$ $$ = \cos x + \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} + \frac{1}{\sin x} + 1 = $$ $$ = \frac{\sin x \cos x + \cos x + 1 + \sin x}{ \sin x } $$ $$ = \frac{(\cos x + 1)(\sin x + 1)}{\sin x} $$

rewrite the denominator

$$ \sin x + \tan x + \sec x + 1 $$ $$ = \sin x + \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} + \frac{1}{\cos x} + 1 = $$ $$ = \frac{\cos x \sin x + \sin x + 1 + \cos x}{\cos x} $$ $$ = \frac{(\cos x + 1)(\sin x + 1)}{\cos x} $$

and finally rewrite integral


$$ = \int \frac{(\cos x + 1)(\sin x + 1)}{\sin x} \cdot \frac{\cos x}{(\cos x + 1)(\sin x + 1)} dx $$ $$ = \int \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} dx $$ $$ = \log{\sin x} + C $$

$$ \int_{0}^{2\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)^{11} dx $$

from Theorem 1

$$ = \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)^{11} dx + \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin{(2\pi - x)} + \cos{(2\pi - x)})^{11} dx $$ $$ = \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)^{11} dx + \int_{0}^{\pi} (-\sin{x} + \cos{x})^{11} dx $$

from Theorem 2

$$ = \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)^{11} dx + \int_{0}^{\pi} (-\sin{(\pi - x)} + \cos{(\pi - x)})^{11} dx $$ $$ = \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)^{11} dx + \int_{0}^{\pi} (-\sin{x} - \cos{x})^{11} dx $$ $$ = \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin x + \cos x)^{11} dx - \int_{0}^{\pi} (\sin{x} + \cos{x})^{11} dx $$ $$ = 0 $$
